# Dawnwatch Source & Evidence Policy

## Evidence classes

### Tier A — Authoritative
Court judgments, official regulator registers/notices, police/prosecutor notices, and official corporate records.

### Tier B — Strong independent reporting
Established investigative journalism, reputable national media, and credible specialist reporting.

### Tier C — Corroborated public evidence
Archived websites, app-store records, company marketing material, and multiple independent public reports.

### Tier D — Unverified
Single anonymous complaint, isolated social post, or screenshot with incomplete provenance.

### Tier E — Weak / potentially manipulated
Anonymous accusation without supporting material, duplicated reposts, coordinated campaigns, or unverifiable media.

## Handling rules

- A source can prove that a statement was made without proving the statement itself.
- Promotional material is authoritative for **what the promoter claims**, not for whether the claim is true.
- Social reports are signals, not adjudications.
- The absence of a licence search result must be phrased precisely: “No matching licence was found in the register searched on [date],” unless an authority has confirmed unlicensed status.
- Charges, allegations, regulator warnings, findings, settlements, acquittals, and convictions must be stored as distinct event types.
- Public pages must preserve publication dates and source provenance.

## User submissions

User evidence remains private by default until reviewed and redacted. Victim names, phone numbers, IDs, account numbers, precise home addresses, and unrelated personal information must not be exposed publicly.

## Corrections

Dawnwatch must preserve a correction log. New verified evidence may reduce or remove an indicator. Prior public states remain auditable through risk snapshots.
